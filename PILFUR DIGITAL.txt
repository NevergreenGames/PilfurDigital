PILFUR
A Semi-Cooperative Heist Dice Game
________________


Overview
Pilfur is a semi-cooperative survival game about planning and executing an impossible heist. Players work together to pull off a single daring job—but when things go wrong, each player must still escape on their own.
Every game of Pilfur represents one heist.
The crew plans boldly, momentum builds, mistakes compound, and the getaway decides who makes it out.
Players may all win.
Some may win while others are caught.
It is also possible for everyone to lose.
Winning and losing are strictly binary. There is no score.
________________


Game Information
* Players: 1–4
* Play Time: 30–45 minutes
* Genre: Light strategy, narrative dice game
* Core Themes: Heists, improvisation, pressure, consequences
________________


Components
* 10 Heist Target Cards
* 1 Phase Card Deck
* Character Cards (one per player)
* Dice
   * d4, d6, and d20 (used as momentum dice)
   * Red d6s (used as heat dice)
________________


Dice Types
* Momentum Dice
   * Any d4, d6, or d20
   * Used during the heist and escape
   * Drawn from a shared supply
   * Considered infinite—use substitutes if needed
* Heat Dice
   * Red d6s only
   * Shared pool
   * No maximum limit
________________


Setup
1. Choose Characters
Each player selects a character card and reads its ability to the group.
2. Deal Phase Cards
Take 12 phase cards and deal them evenly among all players.
This is each player’s starting hand.
3. Draw the Heist Target
Randomly draw 1 heist target card and place it face up in the center of the table.
4. Determine First Player
Randomly choose a starting player. Play proceeds clockwise.
________________


The Heist Structure
Each game of Pilfur proceeds in three phases:
   1. Planning the Heist
   2. Executing the Heist
   3. The Escape
________________


PLANNING THE HEIST
The planning phase establishes the plan—bold, detailed, and doomed to go wrong.
________________


Declare the Target
The starting player reads aloud:
“We’re going to steal the [target name].”
Read any special rules on the heist target card.
________________


Build the Plan
Starting with the first player and proceeding clockwise, each player must do one of the following on their turn:
   * Play one phase card from their hand face up into the center of the table, describing how it contributes to the plan.
Phase cards represent steps in the heist such as distractions, preparations, or obstacles dealt with in advance.
Players may not skip their turn.
________________


Ending the Plan
Instead of playing a phase card, a player may end planning by declaring:
“Then I steal the [target name].”
This immediately ends the planning phase.
   * Other players may not object.
   * If all phase cards have been played, planning must end.
________________


EXECUTING THE HEIST
The plan is put into motion—one phase at a time.
________________


Turn Order
Execution proceeds in the same clockwise order as planning.
Each player resolves their own phase card when it is their turn.
________________


Resolving a Phase
On your turn:
   1. Add your phase card’s momentum dice to the pool
   2. Roll All Momentum Dice in the Pool
   3. Check the Requirement
Compare the roll/dice result to your phase card’s requirement:
      * Examples include:
      * X of a kind
      * = N
      * N+
      * < N
      4. Place dice on your phase card to fulfill its requirement
      * If you satisfied the requirement on your phase card, place the remaining unused dice on the phase cards from previous rounds, completing as many of their requirements as you can.
      * If you didn’t satisfy your card’s requirement this phase was a total bust! (see Total Bust section)
      5. Resolve the Outcome
      * Whether you succeed or fail, play passes to the next player.
      * After the roll, all momentum dice remain in the pool.
________________


Failure and Flashbacks
If a phase roll does not meet its requirement, this is a Total Bust! This phase and all previous rounds’ phase cards are considered failed (each of those players draws a phase card) and all momentum dice are left in the pool and considered unused.
If a phase roll met it’s requirement but didn’t meet all of the previous phase card requirements, the unmet phases are considered failed. Those players each draw a card.

Either way, players then can play flashbacks on any of the failed phase cards.
________________


Flashback Opportunity
Starting with the player to the active player’s left and proceeding clockwise (ending with the active player), any player may attempt a flashback.
To flashback:
      * Play a phase card from your hand onto an unfulfilled phase card.
      * The flashback succeeds only if the already-rolled dice meet that card’s requirement.
      * Then add the new flashback card’s dice to the pool (the previous card’s dice remain in the pool as well)
Important Rules:
      * Only one flashback may be attempted per failed phase card.
      * Flashbacks occur after seeing the dice.
      * Dice are not rerolled.
________________


If a Flashback Succeeds
      * The flashback card’s dice are added to the flashing player’s private stash.
      * The dice used by the flashback are placed on top of it and no longer considered “unused”
________________


If There are any Remaining Momentum Dice
      * Add 1 heat die per unused momentum die to the escape pool.
________________


Heist Completion
Once all planned phases and the final target are resolved, proceed to the escape.
________________


THE ESCAPE
The job is done. Now everyone runs.
________________


Step 1: Reveal Complications
Each player reveals all phase cards remaining in their hand.
These cards are now escape complications along that player’s personal escape route.
________________


Step 2: Resolve Escape Complications
Each player:
      1. Rolls all dice in their private stash.
      2. Assigns rolled dice to their revealed complications.
      * Dice used to complete one complication cannot be used on another.
      * Complications can be completed in any order.
      3. Dice that are not used may be given to another player, but only if:
      * The giving player has resolved all of their own complications.
      * The transfer happens before heat is rolled.
________________


Step 3: Clean Getaway Check
      * If all players resolve all of their escape complications:
      * The entire crew gets out clean.
      * All players win.
      * The game ends.
________________


Step 4: Roll the Heat
If any player has unresolved escape complications:
      1. Roll the entire heat dice pool once.
      2. For each player with unresolved complications:
      * If the heat dice satisfy any one of that player’s unresolved complication requirements, that player is caught and loses.
Players without unresolved complications are safe.
Multiple players may be caught.
________________


End of the Game
      * Any player who is not caught wins.
      * Any player who is caught loses.
      * If all players are caught, everyone loses.
