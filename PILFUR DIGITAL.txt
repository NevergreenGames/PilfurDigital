________________


PILFUR DIGITAL
A Solo Roguelike Heist Dice Game
________________


Overview
Pilfur Digital is a solo, run-based adaptation of the tabletop game Pilfur.
A run is a sequence of five heists. Each heist is its own self-contained
grid puzzle: explore the building, fulfill the right tiles in the right
order, beat the heat to the goal, and walk away with the score before the
heat catches you. Character abilities, charges, and a leveling die carry
between heists; failing any heist ends the run.

Core fantasy: improvise an impossible heist with mediocre dice and a
character who's better at one thing than three.

Win and lose conditions are binary. There is no score across runs — only
"got out clean" or "got caught."
________________


Game Information
* Players: 1 (solo only — multiplayer is not in the digital version)
* Run length: 5 heists, ~30–45 minutes
* Genre: Roguelike dice puzzle, light grid-exploration
* Core themes: Cause-and-effect dice play, accumulating heat, escalating risk
________________


Run Structure
A run is one playthrough, end to end:
   1. Title screen → Play
   2. Pick a character (1 of 3)
   3. Heist 1 → Heist 2 → Heist 3 → Heist 4 → Heist 5
   4. Between heists: ability draft (pick 1 of 3 to add to your loadout)
   5. End: clean getaway (won) or caught/trapped (lost)
A run ends immediately on the first heist you fail. The character die size
and any unspent ability charges persist through to the next heist if you
succeed.
________________


CHARACTERS
The player picks one character at the start of the run.
Each character has:
   * A starting die size (d4 / d6 / d10)
   * One signature ability with a trigger and an effect
   * Flavor text and a unique icon
The starting die — the "character die" — is special: it is part of the
dice pool throughout every heist, it never becomes heat, and if it ever
rolls its maximum value while being consumed it permanently steps up one
size (d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12 → d20). A small d4 can become a d20 by
the end of a run.
Currently shipped:
   * The Hacker — d6 starter, ability "BACKDOOR" (trigger: 3 of a kind,
     effect: spend a charge to set a target die to its max).
   * The Demolitionist — d4 starter, ability "SHAPED CHARGE" (trigger:
     sum ≥ 12 from 2+ dice, effect: spend a charge to reroll the entire
     pool).
   * The Veteran — d10 starter, ability "STEADY HAND" (trigger:
     4-straight, effect: spend a charge to remove 2 heat dice).
________________


THE HEIST
Each heist plays out on a grid representing the building you're robbing.
Heist 1 uses a smaller 5×5 playable grid; heists 2–5 expand to 7×7. Both
sit on top of a hidden "porch row" with a single start tile in the
middle column — the player's spawn — and the tile directly above the
porch is always a guaranteed-open empty square so the first move into the
building is unblocked.

Each tile on the playable grid is one of:
   * Hidden — fogged. Revealed when the player or an adjacent tile is
     stepped on.
   * Revealed phase tile — has a phase card with a requirement and a
     reward (momentum dice).
   * Revealed start / open tile — walkable, no card.
   * Wall — impassable barrier (procedurally placed; may not block
     start-to-target reachability).
   * Goal — the heist target. Has its own requirement card. Reaching it
     wins the heist.
   * Player-fulfilled — a phase tile the player has cleared. Walkable.
   * Heat-fulfilled — a phase tile the heat has clinched. Permanent
     blocker.
   * Void — invisible spacer slot on the porch row, never rendered.
________________


Dice
There are two dice pools per heist:
   * The player's pool — starts each heist with the character die plus a
     d6 starter, and grows as the player fulfills phase tiles that grant
     momentum dice. Every reroll adds one bonus d6 to the pool.
   * Heat — adversary dice. Heat starts each heist at (nodeIndex + 2)
     d6s, so heist 1 begins with 2 heat and heist 5 begins with 6 heat.
     Heat scales further when the heat fulfills phase tiles (their
     momentum dice become new heat).

Dice are unrolled (value=null) until rolled. The first roll fires
automatically when the player enters the heist screen — there is no
ROLL button. Subsequent rerolls fire automatically whenever the player
moves onto a walkable tile or fulfills a phase tile.

Auto-pick bias: when the player clicks a green-glowing tile without
manually selecting dice, the engine automatically picks a satisfying
subset. Among satisfying subsets, the engine prefers ones that include
the character die — keeping it rolling so it has more chances to max-roll
and level up.
________________


REQUIREMENTS
Phase cards and goals require one of three patterns:
   * Sum: dice sum {< | ≤ | = | ≥ | >} target value, optionally with a
     minimum dice count (e.g. "sum ≥ 15 from 4+ dice").
   * X of a kind: N dice showing the same value (pair, three of a kind,
     etc.).
   * Straight: N consecutive integers in a row.
The same patterns are reused for ability triggers.
________________


THE TURN
A heist progresses turn by turn. On each turn:
   1. Dice are rolled (auto on heist start, auto on movement / fulfillment).
   2. The board updates:
      * Tiles glow YELLOW when adjacent and have a phase card. They are
        "selectable" but the requirement is not currently met.
      * Tiles glow GREEN when their requirement could be satisfied right
        now by the dice in the pool — clicking commits the play.
      * Walkable adjacent tiles (start tiles or already-fulfilled tiles)
        glow BLUE with a faint reroll glyph — clicking advances onto the
        tile, ending the turn and triggering a fresh roll.
      * Every other tile is muted so the candidate moves stand out.
   3. Heat looms: any heat dice from the fresh roll that could satisfy an
      adjacent phase tile are RESERVED on that tile (rendered as small
      red dice hovering above it). Reserved dice are removed from the
      heat pool until the intent resolves.
   4. The player acts. A turn is exactly one action:
      * Move onto a walkable adjacent tile. Ends the turn → triggers
        end-of-turn heat resolution and a fresh roll.
      * Fulfill an adjacent phase tile. Spend the satisfying dice (the
        character die returns unrolled instead of being consumed).
        Auto-move onto the cleared tile. Ends the turn → triggers heat
        resolution and a fresh roll. Reaching the goal tile this way
        WINS the heist.
      * Activate an ability. Spends one charge. Does not end the turn.
      * Select dice manually (toggle). Does not end the turn.

End-of-turn heat resolution: every looming heat intent fires. Its tile
becomes heat-fulfilled (locked, impassable), and any reward momentum dice
on that tile become NEW heat dice (joining the heat pool unrolled,
rolling on the next turn). The player can preempt a looming intent by
fulfilling that tile themselves before the turn ends, in which case the
reserved heat dice are released back to the heat pool.
________________


ABILITIES AND CHARGES
Each character starts with one signature ability. Between heists, the
player picks one drafted ability to add to their loadout (or skips).

Abilities are charge-based:
   * After every roll the engine checks each owned ability's TRIGGER
     against the freshly rolled pool. If a satisfying subset exists, the
     ability gains 1 charge.
   * Charges accumulate. They persist across heists.
   * Activating an ability spends 1 charge and applies its EFFECT.

Effects can be:
   * Pool-modifying — reroll all / highest / lowest / selected, set die
     to max / value, duplicate die, set two dice to 1, remove ones, add
     dice to pool.
   * Heat-modifying — remove 1 or 2 heat dice.

Targeting: some effects need 1 specific die ("die"); others need 2
("dice"); the rest are non-targeted. To activate a targeted ability the
player can either pre-select the right number of dice and click ACTIVATE,
or click ACTIVATE first and then click dice to enter "waiting" mode.

Batch fire: if a single-target ability is fired with multiple dice
selected, the ability is repeated once per selected die, consuming one
charge per fire, until either all selected dice are hit or charges run
out. A single click on ACTIVATE with three dice selected and three charges
fires the ability three times.
________________


CAUSE-AND-EFFECT FEEDBACK
Pilfur Digital deliberately telegraphs cause and effect. After every roll:
   * Player and heat dice cups jiggle and the dice tumble inside them.
   * Each ability that gained a charge from the roll plays a celebratory
     pop animation IN ORDER, one at a time. While an ability pops, the
     specific dice in the pool that satisfied its trigger enlarge and
     glow gold so the player sees which dice caused which charge.
   * Tiles repaint based on what is now possible.

After every ability activation:
   * Every die the ability transformed enlarges, glows gold, and jiggles
     for ~750ms — the player sees the effect take hold on those exact
     dice.

After every heat resolution:
   * Heat dice fly from the heat banner onto each tile they're claiming
     before the looming animation begins.
   * On end-of-turn, locked tiles play a heat-lock keyframe.
________________


HEIST OUTCOMES
A heist ends as one of:
   * WON — the player fulfilled the goal tile.
   * CAPTURED — the heat fulfilled the goal tile (or, equivalently,
     heat-fulfilled it on end-of-turn resolution).
   * TRAPPED — the player can no longer reach the goal: a BFS over
     non-permanently-blocked tiles (i.e. excluding walls and
     heat-fulfilled tiles) fails to reach any 8-neighbor of the goal,
     and the player is not currently adjacent to the goal.

A heist-end overlay shows the outcome over the final board state. The
player can minimize it to inspect the layout, then proceeds:
   * WON on a non-final node → ability draft → next heist.
   * WON on the final node → game over (run won).
   * CAPTURED or TRAPPED → run reset to character select.
________________


META-PROGRESSION (within a run)
Persisted across heists:
   * Character die size (level-ups stick).
   * Owned abilities (signature + drafted).
   * Unspent ability charges.
Reset between heists:
   * Pool composition (resets to character die + d6 each heist).
   * Heat (regenerated from the per-node scale).
   * Tile state (each heist generates a fresh grid).
Reset between runs:
   * Everything.
________________


PRESENTATION
The game ships with:
   * A title screen, settings menu, "How to play" overlay, and credits
     view, accessible at any time via a persistent gear icon.
   * Background music with mood stations (jazz / tense / piano / lofi),
     selectable in settings, with smooth crossfades between screens.
   * An interactive tutorial keyed to first-time appearances of mechanics
     (character select, map, first heist, fulfilling, heat looming,
     ability charges, movement reroll, off-screen compass, outcome).
     Each tutorial step is either dismissed by performing the relevant
     action ("auto") or by clicking "Got it" ("info").
   * Explicit emoji icons on every job (target) and ability, surfaced on
     character select, the map, the draft, the ability sidebar, and the
     goal tile itself.
________________


AUDIO / VISUAL POLISH
   * Camera that pans with the player and locks-to-center when the grid
     fits the 5×5 viewport.
   * Off-screen compass dots for the goal and the player when either is
     outside the viewport.
   * Reduce-motion setting that softens animations.
   * Strong visual hierarchy on the grid: green will-succeed tiles
     dominate, blue movable tiles assert, yellow fulfillable-but-not-
     met tiles whisper, every other tile dims.
